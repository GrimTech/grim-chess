# Yusupov's Nine-Book Course (Build Up / Boost / Chess Evolution)

## 1. Author and context

Artur Yusupov (b. 1960) was World Junior Champion in 1977 and later reached world number three. He trained for many years with Mark Dvoretsky and co-authored the *School of Future Champions* series with him (see `soviet-school-botvinnik-dvoretsky-yusupov.md`) [6]. The nine-book course, published by **Quality Chess** from 2008, condenses the Dvoretsky–Yusupov training approach for players below master level. Publisher material states that it won the **FIDE Boleslavsky Medal** for best instructional books (2009) [4]. *This comes from publisher and retailer copy; we did not find a FIDE source.*

## 2. Structure

The course has **three levels with three volumes each** [1][2]:

| Level | Colour | Target (author/publisher) | Volumes, in recommended reading order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fundamentals** | Orange | "up to 1500" | *Build Up Your Chess 1* → *Boost Your Chess 1* → *Chess Evolution 1* |
| **Beyond the Basics** | Blue | "up to 1800" | *Build Up Your Chess 2* → *Boost Your Chess 2* → *Chess Evolution 2* |
| **Mastery** | Green | "up to 2100" | *Build Up Your Chess 3* → *Boost Your Chess 3* → *Chess Evolution 3* |

Jacob Aagaard (Quality Chess) described the plan as "3 [books] of up to 1500, up to 1800 and up to 2100" [1].

**Each volume** [2][3]:
- **24 chapters.** Each chapter has instructional material (typically model positions and annotated examples) followed by a **test** of exercises.
- Exercises are **scored with points**. Harder problems are worth more, and partial credit is given. Each chapter's score maps to a grade band (fail / pass / good / excellent) [3].
- A **final test** covers the whole book. One reviewer reports a maximum of 47 points for the final test of *Build Up Your Chess 1* [3].
- **Topic rotation.** Tactics, strategy, positional play, endgames, calculating variations and openings are distributed across each book, so every volume is a mixed curriculum rather than a single-topic monograph [2][3]. In *Build Up Your Chess 1*, one reviewer counted 11 tactics, 4 endgame, 3 positional, 2 opening, 2 strategy and 2 calculation chapters [3].
- Time per chapter: about **30 minutes to 2 hours** for the lesson, plus a similar time for the test [3].

## 3. Pedagogical features

1. **Explicit spiral curriculum.** Each topic recurs at greater depth in each volume and level.
2. **Retrieval with scoring.** Every chapter ends with a scored test. The scoring gives learners a calibrated self-assessment and a mastery gate: if the score is low, re-study before continuing.
3. **Interleaving at book level.** Chapter topics rotate (tactics → endgame → strategy …), which spaces and interleaves topics, although each chapter is blocked internally.
4. **Worked examples then problems**, which suits the 1200–2000 audience under cognitive load theory (see `../01-learning-science/cognitive-load-theory.md`).
5. **Dvoretsky-style exercise selection**: classic positions chosen for instructive value rather than novelty.

## 4. Calibration: intended vs actual difficulty

Reviewers widely report that the books are **harder than the labels suggest**:
- One reviewer (a WCM) felt *The Fundamentals* suits 1500–1800 rather than under 1500 [3].
- Another notes that many readers think the Orange series is for players up to about 1800, not 1500, and recommends a minimum of about 1400 USCF [2].

**Practical mapping (consensus of reviews; anecdotal):** Orange ≈ 1300–1800, Blue ≈ 1600–2000, Green ≈ 1900–2300.

## 5. Critiques

- **Exercise errors.** Some solutions are flawed by modern engine standards, and reviewers recommend checking with an engine [2].
- **Opening chapters age quickly** and are thin compared with dedicated repertoire works.
- **Self-study feedback** is limited to the printed solution. There is no explanation of *why the learner's alternative fails*. Digital editions (Chessable, Forward Chess) partly address this [5].
- **Volume of work.** 216 chapters plus tests is a multi-year programme. Many learners do not finish. *Anecdotal.*
- **No outcome evaluation** exists. The evidence for efficacy is reviewers' and coaches' reports.

## 6. Lessons for Grim Chess

1. **Copy the unit format.** Instruction, then a scored test with differentiated points and partial credit, then a grade, then a gate. This combines retrieval, feedback and mastery progression.
2. **Mixed-topic units** at every level, so that no level is "all tactics" or "all openings".
3. **Recalibrate levels empirically.** Do not trust author labels. Measure the actual solve rates of learners at each rating and place content accordingly. Yusupov's mismatch is a cautionary example.
4. **Engine-verify all exercise solutions** on ingest, and flag alternative solutions as also correct.
5. **Explain refutations of the learner's own answer** (the engine plus an explanation layer). This is the main advantage over print.
6. **Licensing option.** The series is a strong candidate for licensing as a "classical curriculum" track for about 1400–2200, subject to the recalibration above.

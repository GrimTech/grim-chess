# The Steps Method (Brunia & van Wijgerden)

## 1. Origin and status

- Developed in **1987** in the Netherlands by **Rob Brunia** (an education specialist) and **IM Cor van Wijgerden** (a former national coach of the Dutch federation) [4][6].
- It is the official teaching method of the **Royal Dutch Chess Federation (KNSB)** and is widely used in Dutch, Belgian, German, Austrian and Swiss schools and clubs [4][6].
- The publisher claims more than 400,000 children and young people have learned chess with it [4]. *This is a vendor claim and has not been independently verified.*
- The English editions are published by New in Chess. An online puzzle platform aligned with the Steps levels (Chessity) launched around 2012 [6].

## 2. Structure

The method has **six Steps**, each a graded level [1][4]:

| Component | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Manual for Chess Trainers** (Steps 1–5) | Lesson plans and teaching notes for the trainer, teacher or parent: explanation, questions to ask, common misconceptions [1][2] |
| **Basic Workbook** (one per Step) | The core exercise set for the pupil, practised directly after each lesson [2] |
| **Plus / Extra / Mix workbooks** | Extra exercises at the same level for consolidation, more varied and mixed practice [1][2] |
| **Thinking Ahead** workbooks | Calculation and look-ahead practice at the same level [1] |
| **Stepping Stones** | A slower track for **younger children (about 6–9 years)**, splitting early material into smaller steps [7] |
| **Step 6: Manual for Independent Learners** | Designed for self-study rather than a trainer, covering king attack, tactics, endgames, positional play and opening preparation [3][4] |

Approximate topic flow (from publisher descriptions and secondary summaries [2][3][4][5]):
- **Step 1:** board and pieces, captures, attack and defense, check, **mate in one**, basic rules, simple opening principles.
- **Step 2:** double attack, pins, basic mates and mating patterns, piece value and exchanges.
- **Step 3:** more tactical motifs (eliminating the defender, discovered attack), pawn endings, introductory plans.
- **Step 4:** deeper tactics, positional elements (good and bad pieces, open files, weak squares), endgame technique.
- **Step 5:** strategy (pawn structure, planning), attack on the king, advanced tactics.
- **Step 6:** self-study of complex tactics, attack, endgames and positional play.

**Level mapping (approximate, contested).** No official Step-to-rating table was found. One secondary source describes the full series as taking a learner from beginner to about **2100 Elo** [5]. Others place Step 6 lower. Treat any mapping as indicative only.

## 3. Didactic principles

From the method's documentation and secondary descriptions [4][5]:

1. **Small steps.** Each lesson adds one new idea. Earlier material is assumed and recycled.
2. **Know → understand → apply.** Knowledge transfer is staged: first knowing a rule or pattern, then understanding why, then applying it in play.
3. **Immediate practice.** Each lesson is followed directly by workbook exercises at the same level, and the extra workbooks consolidate before moving on.
4. **Checkmate is delayed.** At the very start, checkmate "plays no part". Children first learn piece movement and capturing through simple games and goals [4]. This reduces cognitive load (see `../01-learning-science/cognitive-load-theory.md`).
5. **Play and analysis are integral.** Pupils play games and discuss them, not only solve sheets [4].
6. **Trainer as questioner.** The manuals favor asking questions over lecturing. This approximates retrieval and generation (see `../01-learning-science/retrieval-practice-interleaving-desirable-difficulties.md`).
7. **Mastery before progression.** Children move to the next Step only after mastering the previous one's workbook material, not by age or calendar.

## 4. Evidence

- **No randomized or controlled outcome study** of the Steps Method was found in our searches.
- Its longevity (since 1987), official federation status and multinational adoption are institutional evidence of practicability and teacher acceptance, **not of efficacy relative to alternatives**.
- The design aligns well with general learning-science principles: worked examples followed by practice, guidance fading, mastery progression, and retrieval through exercises. This gives it **indirect theoretical support**.

**Evidence strength: anecdotal–weak for outcomes; moderate for alignment with learning-science principles (by inference).**

## 5. Strengths

- Exceptionally **fine-grained sequencing**, built for young learners.
- A **high volume of graded exercises** at each level, so pupils consolidate before advancing.
- Clear separation of **trainer material and pupil material**, which supports non-expert teachers such as school teachers and parents.
- Mixed-practice books (Mix, Plus) counteract purely blocked practice.
- Explicit calculation track (Thinking Ahead).

## 6. Weaknesses and critiques

- **Print-centric.** Feedback depends on a trainer checking answers. Self-study learners get delayed feedback (partly remedied by online versions).
- **Slow for adults and older beginners.** Experienced coaches often note that adult learners find early Steps too gradual. *Anecdotal.*
- **Limited opening content** by design. Some parents and pupils want openings earlier.
- **Within-step blocking.** Lessons and their exercises are largely organized by theme, so mixed practice depends on the extra workbooks being used.
- **No adaptive pacing** beyond the trainer's judgment.

## 7. What Grim Chess should borrow

1. **Fine-grained, prerequisite-linked skill graph** for beginners. Each node is one idea and has a mastery threshold before dependent nodes unlock.
2. **Lesson = short explanation → immediate exercises → mixed consolidation → play.**
3. **Delay full-game checkmate goals for young beginners.** Use piece-movement and capture mini-games first (see `../05-children/mini-games.md`).
4. **A separate "thinking ahead" (calculation) strand from the very beginning,** in small doses.
5. **Trainer and parent mode**, with question prompts for adults supervising children.
6. **Consider licensing the Steps content** for the beginner and children's track, or aligning the platform's levels to the Steps for clubs that already use it. This is an interoperability benefit for Dutch and Belgian clubs.

# Age-Banded Recommendations

> **Status note for auditors.** Health-related limits (screen time, sleep, physical activity) come from WHO, AAP and AASM guidance. Chess-specific session lengths and weekly volumes are **design defaults**, informed by developmental research on attention and working memory and by teaching practice (Steps, ChessKid, club coaching). They are not experimentally validated, and the platform will tune them with data.

## Cross-cutting evidence constraints

| Constraint | Source | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Ages 2–4: sedentary screen time ≤ 1 h/day ("less is better") | WHO 2019 [1] | Under-5 screen chess should be minimal. Prefer physical boards with an adult |
| Ages 2–5: limit to about 1 h/day of high-quality programming, co-viewed | AAP 2016 [3] | Parent co-play mode for 4–5 |
| Ages 5–17: ≥ 60 min/day of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity; limit recreational screen time | WHO 2020 [2] | Daily caps and "move break" prompts |
| Sleep: 3–5 y 10–13 h; 6–12 y 9–12 h; 13–18 y 8–10 h (per 24 h) | AASM, Paruthi et al. 2016 [4] | No late-evening play prompts. Quiet hours for minors |
| Sustained attention and working memory grow through childhood | Betts 2006; Gathercole 2004 [5][6] | Shorter blocks and fewer pieces for younger children |
| Early intensive specialization raises burnout risk; sample many activities in childhood | AAP 2016; Côté DMSP [7][8] | No intensive tracks below about 12 without explicit parental opt-in |
| Starting serious chess before about 12–13 predicts higher eventual level | Gobet & Campitelli 2007 [10] | Encourage early, enjoyable exposure, without pressure |

## Band 4–6 ("Explorers")

- **Goals:** enjoyment; board and piece names; how pieces move and capture; simple goals. Full chess rules come later.
- **Content:** mini-games (pawn wars, "capture the stars", single-piece games, king and pawns) (see `mini-games.md`). Checkmate is **delayed**, following Steps [9]. Stories and characters.
- **Format:** **parent or teacher co-play strongly preferred.** Physical board first, app second. Lichess's "Learn" stages show the kind of very short interactive tasks that suit this band [11].
- **Session length (default):** 10–15 minutes total, in 3–5-minute activity chunks. 3–5 sessions/week. *Design default.*
- **Screen time:** within WHO/AAP limits [1][3]. The platform offers a "board-only" printable mode.
- **Competition:** none needed. Informal play with family.
- **Red flags:** frustration, tears, forced sessions. Stop and return to games.

## Band 7–9 ("Builders")

- **Goals:** full rules (castling, en passant, promotion, stalemate); basic mates (Q+K, R+K); simple tactics (forks, pins, skewers); "don't hang pieces"; first notation.
- **Content:** Steps 1–2 or Stepping Stones level [9]; mate-in-one and mate-in-two banks; simple endgames (K+P basics); opening principles only.
- **Format:** short lessons, then exercises, then play (Steps lesson structure). Light gamification with **informational** feedback (see `how-children-learn-chess.md`).
- **Session length (default):** 15–25 minutes, with a break or switch every 8–10 minutes. 3–5 sessions/week. Total about 1–2 h/week for hobby learners. *Design default.*
- **Competition:** scholastic and club events with short time controls are fine if the child wants it. Emphasize fun and sportsmanship. Online play only in child-safe environments (see `online-safety-for-minors.md`).
- **Parent role:** play together, praise effort and process, attend events supportively (see `parental-involvement.md`).

## Band 10–12 ("Climbers")

- **Goals:** tactical fluency; calculation of 2–3-move lines; basic endgame canon (opposition, key squares, Lucena and Philidor introduced); opening repertoire basics; recording and analysing one's own games.
- **Content:** Steps 3–5 / Yusupov Orange level for strong kids; puzzle streams (recognition track); first calculation-track items; guess-the-move on simple classics.
- **Format:** mixed sessions (warm-up puzzles, lesson, practice, game). Introduce **post-game review with self-annotation before engine** (see `../02-methods/game-study-and-analysis-loops.md`).
- **Session length (default):** 30–45 minutes, broken into 10–15-minute activities. Hobby learners: 2–3 h/week. **Ambitious players** (parent opt-in): up to about 5–8 h/week including games. *Design default.*
- **Competition:** regular rated events. Longer time controls (e.g., G/30+ or slower) recommended for learning.
- **Note:** this is the window before the "about 12–13" starting-age threshold seen in the expertise data [10]. Children with strong interest benefit from structured training **without** sacrificing other activities [7][8].

## Band 13–17 ("Competitors")

- **Goals:** depending on ambition, from recreational play to serious competitive development (strategy, deep calculation, endgame technique, opening preparation, tournament psychology).
- **Content:** adult-equivalent tracks by rating (see `../02-methods/synthesis.md`), e.g., Yusupov Blue/Green and later Aagaard. Self-directed planning and monthly diagnosis.
- **Format:** full training plans with autonomy (self-determination). Peer cohorts and camps. Coach access with safeguarding.
- **Session length (default):** 45–90 minutes. Weekly volume by goal: recreational 1–3 h; competitive 5–15 h; elite-track (opt-in, coach-supervised) 15–25+ h. Elite juniors report much higher volumes (see `../01-learning-science/trajectories-and-hours-to-titles.md`). *Design default, with wellbeing checks.*
- **Wellbeing:** sleep 8–10 h [4]; physical activity [2]; tilt management (see `../01-learning-science/motivation-tilt-and-emotional-control.md`); academic balance.
- **Specialization:** from about 13–15, specialization is developmentally more appropriate (DMSP specializing and investment years) [8]. Still monitor for burnout.

## Summary table

| Band | Session (default) | Weekly (hobby) | Weekly (ambitious, opt-in) | Core focus | Competition |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4–6 | 10–15 min (3–5 min chunks) | 30–60 min | — | Mini-games, piece moves | None needed |
| 7–9 | 15–25 min | 1–2 h | 2–4 h | Rules, basic mates, simple tactics | Scholastic / fun |
| 10–12 | 30–45 min | 2–3 h | 5–8 h | Tactics, calculation, basic endgames, own-game review | Rated, slower time controls |
| 13–17 | 45–90 min | 1–3 h | 5–25 h | Full curriculum by rating | Full |

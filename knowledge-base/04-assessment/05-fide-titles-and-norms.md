# FIDE Titles and Norm Requirements

All rules below come from the FIDE Title Regulations effective 1 January 2024 [1][3], unless another source is cited. **Evidence strength: High** (primary regulation text). Annex tables and edge cases should be read in the source itself.

## 1. Titles and rating requirements

| Code | Title | Rating-based award (FIDE rating ever reached) | Norms required | Min. rating for direct title from an eligible event (§0.6.2) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GM | Grandmaster | **2500** (§1.5.3) | Yes: GM norms covering ≥ 27 games | 2300 |
| IM | International Master | **2400** (§1.5.3) | Yes: IM norms covering ≥ 27 games | 2200 |
| FM | FIDE Master | **2300** (§1.3) | No | 2100 |
| CM | Candidate Master | **2200** (§1.3) | No | 2000 |
| WGM | Woman Grandmaster | **2300** (§1.5.3) | Yes | 2100 |
| WIM | Woman International Master | **2200** (§1.5.3) | Yes | 2000 |
| WFM | Woman FIDE Master | **2100** (§1.3) | No | 1900 |
| WCM | Woman Candidate Master | **2000** (§1.3) | No | 1800 |

**Notes**
- Rating-based FM, CM, WFM and WCM titles need the rating to rest on **≥ 30 rated games** for ratings achieved after 1 July 2017 [1].
- The 2024 rule (§0.6.2): when a direct title rests on a rating achieved after 1 January 2024, the player must have played ≥ 30 rated games at that time. Below the minimum, the title is awarded conditionally [1].
- The CM/WCM direct-title rating minimum does not apply at Olympiads [1].
- Direct titles come only from events listed in the *Table for Direct Titles* (§1.2.4), for example continental and world youth championships. A revised table took effect 1 January 2026 [2].

## 2. Norms (GM, IM, WGM, WIM)

| Requirement | GM | IM | WGM | WIM |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Min. performance rating Rp (§1.4.8) | **2600** | **2450** | **2400** | **2250** |
| Min. average opponent rating (§1.4.8a) | 2380 | 2230 | 2180 | 2030 |
| Opponent rating floor (§1.4.6) | 2200 | 2050 | 2000 | 1850 |
| Titled opponents (§1.4.5) | ≥ 1/3 (min 3) GMs | ≥ 1/3 (min 3) IM/GM | ≥ 1/3 (min 3) WGM/IM/GM | ≥ 1/3 (min 3) WIM/WGM/IM/GM |
| Min. score (§1.4.8b) | 35% | 35% | 35% | 35% |

**Common rules** [1]
- **Games.** At least **9 games** per norm (§1.4.1.1). There are exceptions: team events (7–8 games) and the World Cup (8 games counted as 9). A single 8-game norm is allowed if a forfeit or bye occurs in a 9-round event (§1.4.1.3). Events longer than 13 rounds count as only 13 games (§1.7). The norms in a title application must cover **≥ 27 games** in total (§1.5.1).
- **Opponent mix.** ≥ 50% of opponents must be title-holders, CM and WCM excluded (§1.4.5.1). At least two federations other than the applicant's must be represented. At most 3/5 of opponents may come from the applicant's own federation and at most 2/3 from any single federation (§1.4.3–1.4.4). For a 9-round GM norm the Annex example gives 2 foreign federations, 3 GMs, 5 title-holders, at most 6 opponents from one federation and at most 5 from the applicant's own.
- **Rating floor adjustment.** If more than one opponent is rated below the floor, the lowest-rated one is raised to the floor (§1.4.6.3). An unrated opponent counts as 1400 unless the floor applies (§1.4.6.4).
- **Performance.** Rp = Ra + dp, rounded with 0.5 rounding up (§1.4.8–1.4.9).
- **Schedule and time control.** At most 12 hours of play and 2 rounds per day. Each player must have at least 2 hours for a 60-move game. A GM or WGM application needs at least one norm from an event played at one round per day over at least 3 days (§1.1.2–1.1.3a). All games must be played at the same time control (§1.1.3b). The chief arbiter must be an IA or FA (§1.1.5). Events must be registered 30 days in advance (§1.1.1).
- **Since 30 June 2022 (§1.5.6).** Every application containing a norm earned after that date must include at least one norm from a listed type of event, for example a Swiss with ≥ 40 participants averaging ≥ 2000, an Olympiad, or a national championship final [1].
- **2026 time-control pilot.** QC-approved major events at **45+30 or longer** can yield norms, but at most one such norm may count toward any single title [4].

### 2.1 Worked example: performance needed for a GM norm in 9 rounds

With an average opponent rating of 2450, the player needs dp ≥ 150. In the FIDE table p = 0.70 gives 149 and p = 0.72 gives 166. A score of 6.5/9 (p = 0.722) therefore succeeds and 6/9 (p = 0.667, dp ≈ 125) falls short. The logistic approximation dp ≈ 400·log10(p/(1−p)) reproduces these values to within a few points.

## 4. Consequences for Grim Chess (normative)

1. **Real titles cannot be earned on the platform.** Every FIDE title requires FIDE-rated over-the-board results: a FIDE rating for FM/CM/WFM/WCM, plus norms in registered events with arbiters for GM/IM/WGM/WIM [1]. Online platform games are never FIDE-rated.
2. **Milestones are "-equivalent", and are tied to ratings, not norms.** The platform's CM/FM/IM/GM-equivalent milestones are tied to the estimated FIDE-equivalent rating and its confidence bound (`platform-measurement-model.md`). A "norm-equivalent performance" badge may describe a performance rating ≥ 2600 / 2450 / 2400 / 2250 over ≥ 9 games against calibrated opposition averaging ≥ 2380 / 2230 / 2180 / 2030. It must state that bot opponents are not title-holders and that no FIDE norm has been earned.
3. **Naming.** Badge names must not be "GM", "IM" or "FM" alone. Use, for example, "GM-equivalent strength (estimated)".

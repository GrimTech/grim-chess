# Practical Play and Psychology

## Time management

- **Budget**: divide the main time into opening (fast, using prep), middlegame (most of the time; critical positions), and a reserve for the time-control move or endgame. A common classical guideline: be no more than ~1/3 of the time used by move 15 unless the position is critical.
- **Critical moments** deserve long thinks: structural decisions, sacrifices, transitions to endgames, opponent's surprise. Routine moves should be made quickly (Kotov's advice to avoid time trouble by not re-checking the same lines) [2].
- **Increment play** (e.g., 90+30): never let time fall below the level where the increment alone is enough for a safe move. In sudden death (no increment) keep a reserve for quickplay.
- **Time-trouble technique**: simplify the position, make safe moves, use the opponent's time to think, avoid long forcing lines you can't verify, keep score per Art. 8.4 exemption rules (under 5 minutes, increment <30 s) [1].
- **Exploiting opponent's time trouble**: keep the position complicated but safe; avoid giving forcing simplifications.
- Measurable target: no more than X% of games with <1 min per move before move 40 (platform metric by stage).

## Clock handling

- Press the clock with the same hand that moved (Art. 6.2.3); one hand for moves; claims and stopping the clock only per Art. 6.12 and 9.2; flag rules and insufficient material Art. 6.9 [1].
- Blitz/bullet: premoves (online), mouse/touch speed, "flag" strategies in drawn positions, "hand-eye" coordination.
- Rapid vs blitz differences: in rapid, keep thinking time for 2-3 key decisions; in blitz, play intuitively, rely on pattern and simple repertoire.

## Tournament preparation

- **Physical**: sleep, nutrition, hydration, exercise; for long events plan rest days; avoid late-night preparation marathons.
- **Chess preparation**: fresh opening review, tactical warm-up (easy puzzles, not hard calculations) before the round, endgame refreshers.
- **Logistics**: arrive before default time (zero tolerance by default, Art. 6.7) [1]; know the event's regulations (draw-offer restrictions, devices policy Art. 11.3).
- **Routines**: pre-game routine, during-game routine (blunder check, sitting posture, deep breaths), post-game routine (short analysis, then rest).

## Opponent preparation

- Database search for the opponent's games (ChessBase, Lichess/Chess.com profiles for juniors); identify repertoire, recent lines, preferred structures, strengths/weaknesses (time trouble? endgames? tactics?).
- Choose a line that leads to positions the opponent dislikes, or avoid the opponent's preparation with a sideline.
- Stage 5+: prepare 1-2 specific lines; stage 7-8: full engine-assisted preparation with novelties.

## Emotional regulation and tilt

- **Tilt**: degraded decision quality after a loss or a mistake (anger, revenge playing, rushing). Signals: playing faster, rematch impulses, ignoring blunder checks.
- Remedies: stop-loss rules online (e.g., stop after 2 consecutive losses), breathing and reset rituals at the board after a mistake ("the game starts again now"), objective reassessment of the position.
- Rowson's "seven deadly chess sins": thinking, blinking, wanting, materialism, egoism, perfectionism, looseness - a framework for self-diagnosis [4].

## Resilience after mistakes

- Evaluate the new position objectively (often it is still defendable); avoid "resignation thinking"; set practical problems for the opponent. Stage 4+.

## Swindles

- In lost positions, maximise practical chances: create complications, keep pieces on, aim for stalemate tricks, perpetual check, fortress, or traps that look natural for the opponent to fall into; play quickly when the opponent is low on time.
- Swindle typology (useful for lessons): stalemate traps; perpetual check; fortress building; counter-attack against the king; desperado sequences; time-pressure traps.
- Ethics: no gamesmanship that violates Art. 11.5 (distraction).

## Draw offers and claims

- Offer correctly: after making a move, before pressing the clock (Art. 9.1.2.1); cannot be withdrawn; record "(=)" [1].
- Decide by objective evaluation + tournament situation + time on clocks + opponent strength. Repeated offers can be penalised as annoyance (Art. 11.5).
- Know how to claim 3-fold and 50-move draws (write intended move, call the arbiter), and that fivefold/75-move draws are automatic (Art. 9.6) [1].
- Sofia rules / no-draw-before-move-N events: draws only via arbiter.

## Resigning

- Resign in hopeless positions against competent opposition; at low levels, play on (the opponent may not know the technique - e.g., K+Q vs K stalemate risk).

## Post-game analysis

- Analyse **without engine first** (own thoughts, critical moments), then with the opponent if possible (post-mortem), then engine verification; record recurring mistakes in a personal error log (tactical oversights, time, opening, endgame, evaluation). Platform exercise type `game-review`.
- Classify errors by type and phase; feed them to spaced-repetition puzzle sets ("puzzles from your own games").

## Online-specific practice

- Rating systems (Glicko-2 on Lichess; Chess.com Glicko), playing pool differences vs FIDE ratings; premove use; avoid excessive bullet for improvement; anti-cheating conduct; fair-play policies.

## Practical competencies by stage

| Stage | Practical competency |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Use the clock, basic sportsmanship, touch-move, no premature resignation, finish games |
| 3 | Scoresheet, time budget (no flag losses in 15+10), claims, post-game review habit |
| 4 | Time allocation to critical moments, tilt management, stop-loss rules, standard tournament routine |
| 5 | Opponent preparation basics, swindling, defending worse positions, draw-offer decisions |
| 6 | Full tournament preparation, managing multi-round events, handling time trouble consistently |
| 7-8 | Professional routines, deep opponent prep, match strategy, psychology of colour and standings, physical training |

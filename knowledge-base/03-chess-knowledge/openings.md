# Openings

## Opening principles

1. Fight for the centre (pawns on e4/d4/e5/d5 or control from the flanks).
2. Develop minor pieces toward the centre; knights before bishops is the usual default.
3. Castle early; connect rooks; rooks to central or half-open files.
4. Don't move the same piece twice or bring the queen out early without concrete reason.
5. Don't grab pawns if it costs development, unless concretely justified.
6. Every move should have a purpose tied to the structure you're aiming for.
7. Know the **plans of the middlegame structure** your opening leads to - "learn ideas, not only moves".

Stage 1-2. Exceptions taught at stage 4+: hypermodern openings, gambits, delayed castling, early queen moves (Scandinavian, Alapin ...Qxd5).

## ECO classification

The **Encyclopaedia of Chess Openings** (Chess Informant) divides openings into five volumes A-E, each with codes 00-99 (500 codes) [1][2][3]. The Lichess chess-openings dataset maps ~3,500 named lines to ECO codes and is the platform's machine source for opening names [2].

| Volume | Scope |
|---|---|
| A | Flank openings, 1.d4 without 2.c4 ...d5 main lines, Indian systems other than Nimzo/QID/KID/Grünfeld, Benoni, Dutch |
| B | Semi-open games other than the French: 1.e4 with replies other than 1...e5 / 1...e6 (Sicilian, Caro-Kann, Pirc, Alekhine, Scandinavian, Modern) |
| C | 1.e4 e5 open games and the French Defence |
| D | Closed games 1.d4 d5 (Queen's Gambit) and the Grünfeld |
| E | Indian defences with 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 e6 / g6: Catalan, Bogo-Indian, Queen's Indian, Nimzo-Indian, King's Indian |

## Opening traps and miniatures

Every learner at stage 2-3 should know (by both sides): Scholar's mate defence; Fool's mate; Légal's mate; Fried Liver attack (Two Knights, C57); Noah's Ark trap (Ruy Lopez); Fishing Pole (Ruy Lopez ...Ng4/...h5); Elephant trap (QGD ...Nxd5? Bxd8 Bb4+); Lasker trap (Albin); Englund gambit trap; Blackburne-Shilling trap; Siberian trap (Smith-Morra); Rubinstein trap (QGD); Mortimer trap; Magnus Smith trap (Sicilian); Monticelli trap (Bogo/QID); Cambridge Springs trap; Halosar trap (Blackmar-Diemer). Lichess theme: `opening` puzzles.

## Gambits for beginners

Learning gambits trains development, initiative, and tactics around an exposed king [4]:

| Gambit | Side | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Danish Gambit (C21) | White | Extreme development lead, two bishops raking f7/g7 |
| Evans Gambit (C51-C52) | White | Classical open-game play, d4 centre with tempo |
| Scotch Gambit (C44) / Two Knights | White | Tactics on f7 |
| King's Gambit (C30-C39) | White | Open f-file, king safety trade-offs |
| Smith-Morra (B21) | White vs Sicilian | Open c/d files, development |
| Göring Gambit (C44) | White | Like Danish via Scotch |
| Albin Countergambit (D08-D09) | Black vs QG | Active play, Lasker trap |
| Budapest (A51-A52) | Black vs 1.d4 Nf6 2.c4 | Piece activity, traps |
| Benko Gambit (A57-A59) | Black | Long-term positional compensation (stage 4+) |
| Marshall Attack (C89) | Black in Ruy Lopez | Elite-level sound gambit (stage 6+) |

Rule: at stage 2-3, use gambits in training games to learn initiative; at stage 4+ prefer objectively sound repertoires, keeping gambits as surprise weapons.

## Repertoire building by level

| Stage | Guidance |
|---|---|
| 1 (0-600) | No memorised repertoire. Principles only; 1.e4 e5 open games to learn tactics; how to meet early Qh5/Bc4 attacks. |
| 2 (600-1000) | White: 1.e4 with Italian or Scotch; Black: 1...e5 vs 1.e4 (Two Knights or Giuoco), 1...d5 vs 1.d4 (QGD-style). Learn 5-8 moves of main ideas, all common traps. |
| 3 (1000-1400) | Fix one White system and two Black defences; 8-10 moves in main lines, know the typical middlegame plan and 2-3 model games per line; review every game's opening against the database. |
| 4 (1400-1800) | Build a complete but compact repertoire (e.g., White 1.e4 Italian + Alapin vs Sicilian + Advance vs French/Caro; Black Caro-Kann or 1...e5 plus QGD/Nimzo or Slav). 10-15 moves in main lines, structures and plans, model games. Spaced-repetition drills (Chessable-style MoveTrainer [5]). |
| 5 (1800-2100) | Deepen to 15-20 moves in main lines; add second options to avoid predictability; learn by structure (IQP, Carlsbad, Maroczy). Use engines to check novelties; database prep for opponents. |
| 6 (2100-2300) | Theoretically sound main lines; 20+ moves in critical lines; prepared answers to all common sidelines; personal opening files (ChessBase/Lichess studies) with evaluations. |
| 7 (2300-2450) | Professional repertoire: critical main lines, regular engine-assisted updates from top-level games, preparation against specific opponents, novelties stored. |
| 8 (2450-2700) | Elite level: broad, multi-line repertoire with both 1.e4 and 1.d4 expertise; deep engine/neural-net prep (often 25-30+ moves in forcing lines); knowledge of current top-level trends; ability to deviate and play original positions. |

## How much theory GMs know

- There is no rigorous published count. Evidence-based statements suitable for an auditable KB:
  - Expertise research attributes much of master skill to tens of thousands of stored patterns ("chunks"), only part of which is opening theory (Simon & Gilmartin 1973 estimated 10,000-100,000 chunks for masters; Gobet & Simon refined this into larger "templates") [6].
  - Professional GMs maintain personal preparation databases; in forcing main lines (Najdorf, Grünfeld, Marshall, Semi-Slav) top players' preparation commonly extends to move 25-30+ and occasionally to the endgame, verified with engines [7].
  - In quiet lines, GMs rely on structural understanding: they know typical plans, piece placements and model games rather than long move sequences.
  - Below 2000, opening knowledge correlates weakly with results compared to tactics and endgame technique; most games are decided by tactical errors (Heisman; Lichess/Chess.com accuracy data).
- Platform implication: opening depth targets in `skill-graph.json` are expressed as "moves in main lines + plans + model games", scaled by stage, never as a fixed line count.

## Transition from opening to middlegame

- Recognising the resulting structure, choosing the plan, typical piece regroupings, and the pawn breaks (see `strategy.md#pawn-structure`). Stage 4-6.

## Opening preparation workflow

1. Choose openings fitting style and time budget.
2. Build the skeleton (main line + sidelines) in a study / repertoire tool.
3. Annotate key positions with plans and typical tactics.
4. Drill with spaced repetition (exercise type `opening-drill`).
5. Play training games / blitz in the line; review against database and engine.
6. Update after every tournament game (repertoire maintenance).

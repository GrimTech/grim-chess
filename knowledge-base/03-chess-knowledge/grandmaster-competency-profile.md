# Grandmaster Competency Profile

## Purpose and caveats

- Defines **observable** competencies per band so the platform can (a) place learners, (b) set stage exit exams, and (c) let auditors check that the curriculum reaches GM level.
- Ratings are FIDE classical. Online ratings differ (Lichess ratings typically run a few hundred points above FIDE at club level; the Steps Method author suggests ~+200-300 for Lichess [5]). Puzzle ratings below are Lichess puzzle ratings and are **platform calibration targets, not published norms**.
- FIDE titles: GM requires a 2500 rating at some point plus norms (2600+ performance over 27+ games) [1]; IM 2400; FM 2300; CM 2200.

## Summary matrix

| Domain | ~1400 | ~1800 | ~2200 | ~2500 (GM) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Blunders | Hangs pieces ~1 in 3 games; misses 1-2 move threats | Rarely hangs pieces; misses 2-3 move combos for opponent | Errors mostly evaluative or deep-tactical | Errors are deep, subtle, often time-pressure related |
| Tactics | Knows basic motifs (forks, pins, skewers, back rank); solves 1-2 movers | Solves 3-4 move combinations, sees quiet first moves sometimes | Solves 5+ move combos, defensive resources, intermezzi routinely | Sees combinations as patterns, finds only-moves and quiet moves in complex positions, evaluates sacrifices intuitively |
| Calculation | 2-4 plies, one line | 4-8 plies, 2-3 candidate moves | 8-12 plies, branching tree, comparison method | 10-20 plies in forcing lines, accurate final evaluations, prophylactic calculation, efficient pruning |
| Strategy | Opening principles; vague plans | Knows key structures (IQP, open files, outposts, good/bad bishop) | Structure-based plans, prophylaxis, positional sacrifices | Deep structural judgement, dynamic vs static trade-offs, exchange sacrifices, "feel" for initiative |
| Endgames | KQ/KR mates, basic opposition | Lucena, Philidor, key squares, triangulation, OCB basics | Vancura, R vs minors, fortresses, B+N mate, Q vs R, rook endings with many pawns | DEM-level theory, flawless technique, fortress creation, endgame strategy under pressure |
| Openings | 5-8 moves, traps | 10-15 moves main lines, plans | 15-25 moves, sound repertoire, prepares for opponents | 20-30+ moves in critical lines, engine-assisted novelties, broad repertoire |
| Practical | Occasionally flags; little time control | Uses time reasonably; some tilt | Manages time trouble, opponent prep | Professional routines, deep psychological and tournament strategy |

## Band 1400 club player

What they know/do (and what a <1000 player does not):
- Knows all rules incl. castling, en passant, draw rules; uses a clock and notation.
- Recognises single tactical motifs in puzzles (fork, pin, skewer, discovered attack, back-rank mate, removing the defender); Lichess puzzle rating ~1400-1600.
- Mates with K+Q and K+R; knows basic opposition and the rule of the square.
- Develops pieces, castles, follows opening principles; has a simple repertoire (e.g., Italian, 1...e5, QGD).
- Typical weaknesses: "hope chess" (no systematic blunder check), single-candidate thinking, passive pieces, ignores opponent's plan, poor endgame technique (can't win K+P vs K reliably in all cases).

Exit test to 1800 band (platform): see skill-graph stage 4 criteria.

## Band 1800 strong club player

Beyond 1400:
- Systematic blunder check and CCT for both sides; rarely loses to one-move tactics.
- Solves 3-4 move combinations; knows all Lichess mate themes; recognises Greek gift and typical sacrifices; puzzle rating ~1900-2100.
- Calculates 2-3 candidate moves 4-8 plies deep; uses the elimination method.
- Strategic vocabulary: pawn islands, isolated/doubled/backward pawns, IQP plans, outposts, open files, 7th rank, good/bad bishops, space, minority attack basics.
- Endgames: Lucena, Philidor, key squares, triangulation, Réti, outside passer, Q vs P on 7th, OCB drawing tendency.
- Openings: a full repertoire with 10-15 moves and typical plans; knows traps and model games.
- Practical: avoids most time trouble; reviews games.
- Typical weaknesses: insufficient prophylaxis, misjudged transitions to endgames, superficial evaluation at the end of calculation, weak defence, limited endgame theory (Vancura, R vs minors), predictable play.

## Band 2200 expert / candidate master

Beyond 1800:
- Combinations of 5+ moves including quiet moves; anticipates opponent's tactical resources; puzzle rating ~2300-2500.
- Calculation: branching trees, comparison method, prophylactic thinking, critical-moment detection; rarely leaves calculation mid-exchange.
- Strategy: plans by structure for 15-25 common structures (Carlsbad, Maroczy, Hedgehog, KID, French, Sicilian), prophylaxis, exchange sacrifices, colour complexes, two weaknesses.
- Endgames: Vancura, short/long side, R vs B/N, B vs N, Q vs R, K+B+N mate, fortress concepts, many-pawn rook endings; converts winning endings reliably.
- Openings: theoretically sound repertoire with 15-25 moves in main lines; prepares for opponents with databases.
- Practical: consistent time management; resilience; swindling technique.
- Typical weaknesses (vs IM/GM): lower depth/accuracy in complex calculation, imprecise evaluation of dynamic compensation, inconsistent technique, less opening depth, more errors in time trouble.

## Band 2500 grandmaster

Beyond 2200 (what a GM does that a 2200 does not):
1. **Calculation accuracy and efficiency**: calculates long forcing lines correctly with sidelines; evaluates final positions accurately; prunes irrelevant branches early; finds defensive only-moves; sees quiet moves and in-between moves for both sides (Aagaard's GM Preparation level) [3].
2. **Intuition**: correct first impressions in most positions; quick recognition of thousands of strategic patterns (chunks/templates) [4].
3. **Dynamic judgement**: correct assessment of initiative vs material, exchange sacrifices, long-term compensation; willingness to sacrifice "positionally".
4. **Prophylaxis as a habit**: every move considers the opponent's best plan (Dvoretsky's prophylactic thinking).
5. **Technique**: converts advantages with minimal risk; knows when to simplify; uses "do not hurry" and two-weakness principles.
6. **Endgame theory**: DEM-level knowledge; constructs and breaks fortresses; plays technical endings near-perfectly with little time.
7. **Openings**: broad, engine-checked repertoire; deep prep (often 25-30+ moves in forcing lines); understands resulting middlegames deeply; can play many structures from both sides.
8. **Defence**: tenacious defence in worse positions, practical drawing techniques, counterplay creation.
9. **Practical and psychological mastery**: time management, opponent-specific strategy, tournament situation management, recovery after losses; physical conditioning for long events.
10. **Self-training**: independent analysis, building training material, honest error diagnosis.

## Stage exit tests

| Stage exit | Test battery (platform) |
|---|---|
| 1 -> 2 | Rules quiz 100%, coordinate drill 95%, mate-in-1 90%, KQ v K vs bot |
| 2 -> 3 | 1-move tactics 90% (puzzles ~800-1000), KR v K, castling/en passant/draw quiz, opening principles in play vs bot ~800 |
| 3 -> 4 | Puzzles ~1400 at 85%, opposition/key squares drills, Q v P on 7th, beat bot ~1300 in 15+10 |
| 4 -> 5 | Puzzles ~1800 at 80%, Lucena/Philidor both sides vs engine, repertoire drills 90%, game-review error rate target |
| 5 -> 6 | Puzzles ~2100 at 75%, Vancura/R v minors/K+B+N mate, structure plans quiz, calculation trees |
| 6 -> 7 | Puzzles ~2300 at 70%, fortress recognition, complex rook endings vs engine, deep opening drills |
| 7 -> 8 | Puzzles ~2500 at 65%, GM-Preparation-level calculation exercises, full DEM test positions, performance >2450 in rated play |
| 8 | Performance rating 2500+ over 27+ games vs titled opposition (FIDE norm conditions) |
